# PhD Application Prep

Turn scattered application records into a reliable, reusable evidence base and a target-specific evidence brief. Treat preparation as an audit: a polished claim is unusable until its source and status are clear.

## Choose the needed mode

- **Inventory:** map available files, versions, dates, languages, and document types.
- **Fact bank:** extract claims and attach provenance, confidence, and safe wording.
- **Gap audit:** find contradictions, unsupported metrics, missing records, and questions only the applicant can answer.
- **Target brief:** connect a verified target's requirements and research agenda to the applicant's strongest evidence.
- **Evidence handoff:** prepare document-specific facts, constraints, and decisions for a separate writing workflow.

Run only the modes needed for the request. For output structures and status labels, read [references/artifact-schema.md](references/artifact-schema.md). For the final review before handing material to a writer, read [references/quality-gates.md](references/quality-gates.md).

## Evidence rules

1. Distinguish source facts from interpretation. Never convert an inference into a biographical fact.
2. Give consequential claims a traceable source: degrees, dates, grades, rank, publications, employment, project ownership, metrics, awards, language scores, and named methods.
3. Label each item `verified`, `user-stated`, `inferred`, `conflicting`, or `unknown`. `Verified` requires a supplied record or an authoritative external source.
4. Preserve exact figures and qualifications. Do not recompute or translate grading scales unless the institution supplies the conversion or the result is explicitly labeled as a calculation.
5. Separate personal contribution from team or project impact. Repository stars, group results, and paper-level metrics do not establish individual ownership.
6. Separate completed work, work in progress, forthcoming work, and proposed future work.
7. When sources disagree, retain both versions, cite both locations, and ask for resolution. Do not silently choose the more favorable version.
8. Record a concise `safe wording` for high-risk facts so later drafts do not reintroduce an overclaim.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish scope

Identify the application cycle, degree type, target geography, intended research directions, deliverables, deadlines, and output location when known. Do not block on information that can be recorded as unknown.

### 2. Inventory before interpreting

List candidate-provided records and distinguish originals from extracted text, renders, summaries, and earlier drafts. Prefer originals for verification; use extracts for search and cite their source file.

Do not treat an earlier CV, letter, or profile as independent confirmation of its own claims. It is a claim source, not proof.

### 3. Build the fact bank

Extract atomic claims rather than prose paragraphs. For each claim, record its status, source locator, confidence, relevance, risk, and safe wording. Group claims into identity/contact, education, research, employment, outputs, projects, teaching/service, skills, languages, motivation, and constraints.

### 4. Run the gap audit

Prioritize gaps that could change eligibility, credibility, or positioning. Typical examples include unexplained timelines, unsupported performance numbers, unclear authorship or module ownership, missing third referees, language-test thresholds, and planned methods described as established expertise.

Ask the applicant focused questions in small batches. Explain which document or claim each answer will unlock.

### 5. Verify the target when one is supplied

Use current, authoritative program, department, funding, and supervisor sources. Record the access date. Separate binding requirements from inferred preferences. If current information cannot be checked, mark it unverified rather than relying on memory.

Map each important target criterion to one of: strong evidence, partial evidence, transferable evidence, unsupported, or disqualifying uncertainty. A thematic resemblance is not evidence of fit.

### 6. Produce the evidence handoff

Create a compact factual brief containing:

- the defensible one-sentence candidate positioning;
- two or three evidence-backed narrative routes, ranked for this target;
- a requirement-to-evidence map;
- document-specific keep, omit, verify, and explain decisions;
- approved facts and safe wording;
- unresolved questions and prohibited claims;
- source links or file locators for fact checking.

Preserve tension and specificity in the applicant's history. Do not force every experience into one artificial theme.

## Deliverable boundaries

- Default to creating or updating reviewable Markdown artifacts in the user's chosen workspace; do not overwrite source records.
- Keep sensitive personal information out of new summaries unless it is needed for the application task.
- Do not contact supervisors, referees, institutions, or recommenders without explicit authorization.
- Do not present admission probability as objective fact. If asked to assess competitiveness, state criteria, evidence, and uncertainty.
- Do not draft, rewrite, translate, or polish CV bullets, research proposals, statements, application letters, emails, or other submission prose.
- If writing is requested together with preparation, finish the requested preparation artifacts and clearly hand the writing portion to a separate workflow. Do not silently expand this skill's scope.
