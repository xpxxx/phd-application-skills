# Document playbooks

Read only the section for the requested document. Target instructions override these defaults.

## CV or academic CV

### Purpose

Make relevant qualifications easy to verify quickly. A CV is not a compressed motivation letter.

### Rules

- Preserve exact chronology, official degree and role names, publication status, and current contact details.
- Lead bullets with the applicant's actual action. Separate method, scope, and outcome.
- Use numbers only when the preparation artifacts provide a locator and approved wording.
- Attribute team outcomes and project metrics; do not turn repository popularity or group results into individual impact.
- Keep unpublished manuscripts, work in progress, forthcoming outputs, and publications in clearly distinct categories.
- Tailor ordering and selection to the target, but do not rename experience to mimic vacancy keywords.
- Prefer concrete methods and artifacts over uncheckable adjectives such as “excellent,” “advanced,” or “extensive.”
- When space is limited, omit weak evidence rather than compressing away qualifications.

### Sidecar claim map

Map each consequential bullet to its fact IDs and decision IDs. Flag bullets whose evidence is only `user-stated`.

## Research proposal, research statement, or statement of research interests

### First determine the genre

- A **research proposal** defines a future problem, questions, methods, feasibility, risks, and expected contribution.
- A **research statement** connects prior work, current agenda, and future direction.
- A **statement of research interests** may be shorter and less methodologically complete.

Do not invent a full proposal when the target asks only for a motivation letter or does not request an RP.

### Rules

- Separate prior evidence, interpretation, and proposed future work explicitly.
- Base the problem framing and literature claims on current primary sources. Verify citations; never fabricate a reference, DOI, quotation, or venue detail.
- Show fit through a researchable problem and suitable methods, not supervisor-name repetition or keyword matching.
- Convert interests into questions only after the applicant confirms the intended direction.
- Give each proposed method a reason, needed data, evaluation plan, major risk, and feasible fallback where space permits.
- Treat preliminary experience as feasibility evidence only within its verified scope.
- Avoid claiming access to enterprise data, collaborators, infrastructure, or participants unless confirmed.
- Keep expected contributions prospective: “would investigate,” “aims to,” or equivalent, not “shows” or “establishes.”

### Academic-humanizer mode

Apply the full relevant academic/proposal workflow. Preserve vision where appropriate, but attach feasibility evidence to each ambitious promise. Recheck every citation, number, and past-result claim afterward.

## Application, motivation, or cover letter

### Purpose

Explain why this specific opportunity is being pursued, why the applicant is prepared for it, and what research direction the applicant genuinely wants to develop.

### Rules

- Confirm the addressee, required direction choice, document constraints, and mandatory disclosures.
- The opening must be specific to the target but need not be theatrical. Do not invent a childhood origin story, lifelong passion, emotional turning point, or personal connection.
- Use two or three evidence-backed examples rather than listing the entire CV.
- Make the applicant's individual contribution explicit without erasing collaborators.
- Tie each fit claim to a target requirement, research question, method, or environment that has been verified.
- State gaps honestly when relevant, but do not turn the letter into a self-critique.
- Confirm every motivation sentence that cannot be derived from documented choices. Motivation is personal testimony, not an inference from keywords.
- End with the intended contribution and research direction; avoid generic praise of the institution.
- If AI-use disclosure is required, describe actual assistance, applicant verification, and applicant responsibility. Do not claim “no AI use” when this workflow was used.

### Humanization scope

Use a natural first-person applicant voice. Apply the humanizer's evidence discipline and anti-hype checks, but do not rewrite the letter into third-person academic prose or generic polished sentiment.
