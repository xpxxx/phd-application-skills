# Confirmation gate

Use this gate before writing or materially revising submission prose. Its purpose is to obtain real applicant decisions, not to ask the applicant to approve facts that are already well documented.

## 1. Select only document-relevant items

Read the latest preparation artifacts and classify candidate content into:

| Bucket | Treatment before drafting |
|---|---|
| Verified and target-relevant | May be used within its safe wording; surface only when strategically sensitive. |
| User-stated and low risk | May be used conservatively; include in the decision log. |
| User-stated and high risk | Ask the applicant before prominent use. High risk includes personal ownership, exact impact, authorship, role division, dates, and publication status. |
| Inferred | Present as a proposed interpretation and request approval; never convert it into biography. |
| Conflicting | Must be resolved, transparently qualified, or omitted. |
| Unknown | Ask only if the document needs it; otherwise omit. |
| Prohibited by the preparation brief | Do not use unless new primary evidence causes the preparation artifact to be formally updated. |

Do not dump every open item from the gap audit on the applicant. Ask only what can change the current document.

## 2. Prepare a short confirmation packet

Before asking questions, show a compact packet with:

- document and target;
- proposed reader takeaway;
- two or three evidence blocks planned for emphasis;
- claims or explanations proposed for omission;
- the items that require applicant confirmation.

Distinguish a factual confirmation from a strategic preference. Do not phrase a suggested interpretation as though the applicant already chose it.

## 3. Ask in small batches

Ask no more than three focused questions at once. Each question should state which section or claim it unlocks. Prefer concrete prompts such as:

- “What did you personally implement, and what did the collaborator implement?”
- “Which of these two reasons accurately explains why you chose Direction 1?”
- “May the draft describe this as an unpublished manuscript, or should it be omitted?”

Avoid leading questions that encourage a more flattering but inaccurate answer. When the answer introduces a new consequential fact, request a record or label it `user-stated`.

## 4. Record decisions

Create or update a target-specific Markdown decision log, normally under:

```text
application-writing/<target>/decision-log.md
```

Record:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Decision ID | Stable identifier |
| Date | Confirmation date |
| Document scope | CV, RP, letter, or multiple |
| Question or issue | What required a decision |
| Applicant answer | Concise, faithful record; do not embellish |
| Evidence status | Verified, user-stated, inferred-approved, omitted, or unresolved |
| Allowed wording boundary | Strongest permitted representation |
| Source or conversation locator | Where the answer came from |

Do not treat an old decision as permanent when new evidence contradicts it or the applicant revises it.

## 5. Pass conditions

Drafting may begin when:

- all central claims are verified or explicitly confirmed for use;
- relevant conflicts are resolved, qualified, or intentionally omitted;
- the applicant has approved the positioning and document-specific emphasis;
- required target choices, such as research direction or AI-use disclosure, are resolved;
- no blocking eligibility or identity issue is being concealed by prose.

If the gate does not pass, continue useful preparation work and ask the next focused batch. Do not produce a deceptively complete draft.
